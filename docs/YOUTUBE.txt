Mhizha is an offline agronomy assistant for Zimbabwean smallholder farmers. It cites what it used, and refuses what it cannot source.

Built for the Africa Deep Tech Challenge 2026, Laptop LLM track.

A farmer deciding what to plant this week has no signal in the field, and the nearest AGRITEX extension officer is a bus ride away. For that user the dangerous failure is not unhelpfulness, it is confidence: a wrong spray rate destroys a season, or harms the person holding the sprayer. So Mhizha is built to refuse. A dose that is not written, word for word, in a passage a human has signed off is never emitted, and below its confidence threshold the model is not called at all.

Everything on screen is a real run. The pauses before output are the pauses the machine actually took, and nothing is sped up.

The corpus is a placeholder. The retrieval, safety and validation pipeline is real; the agronomic content is not sourced yet, every answer carries a banner saying so, and what is still needed and which organisations hold it are registered in data/SOURCES.md. We built the pipeline. We did not author agronomy, and we did not write plausible-sounding facts to fill the gaps.

The narration is a synthetic voice, Kokoro-82M. No human read this script aloud.

We submit Qwen3.5 2B in Q4_K_M, template-baked, chosen by a candidate-blind read of the transcripts: three arms labelled A, B and C, the mapping sealed before a single score existed. The blind changed the answer.

Attributions and licences for every component, including the synthetic voice, are in CITATIONS.md in the repository.
